Do you remember playing on a tiny handheld console and feeling amazed by what such limited hardware could achieve?

The Game Boy Advance offered a 240-by-160-pixel screen and resources that look almost impossibly small today. Yet developers built complete worlds for it: readable streets, moving traffic, missions, sound and the illusion of a city that continued beyond the edge of the display.

I played _Grand Theft Auto_ on the GBA when I was a child. The graphics were simple and the viewpoint was distant, but none of that reduced the sense of freedom. The console became a doorway into a place where I could explore, make a mess and invent my own stories between one mission and the next.

That is one of the pleasures of returning to old games. Technical limitations are obvious, but they also reveal the choices behind the experience. Every animation, sound and piece of the map had to earn its place. What survives is not realism; it is the feeling the game managed to create.

Years later, that little city still feels larger in memory than many worlds rendered with far more power. Sometimes imagination was the most important hardware in the console.
